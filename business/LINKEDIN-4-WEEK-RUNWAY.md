## Week 1

### POST #1 — The analytics gap
> Most clinic owners can tell you last month's revenue.
>
> Almost none can tell you which service actually earned it.
>
> The data is right there — in your booking system, your billing, your spreadsheets. But reading it properly takes time you don't have, and hiring a data analyst costs more than the insights are worth to a small practice.
>
> So the big calls — pricing, staffing, which treatments to push — get made on gut feel.
>
> Gut feel is the most expensive line item you can't see on any report.
>
> You don't need a data team. You need the two or three answers a data team would give you.
>
> What's the one number about your practice you wish you understood better?
>
> #clinicmanagement #smallbusiness #dataanalytics

### POST #2 — Three numbers every clinic should check
> 3 numbers every clinic owner should check every month (most only look at #1):
>
> 1️⃣ Revenue by service — not just the total. Where the money actually comes from is almost never where owners think.
>
> 2️⃣ New vs returning patients — one number tells you whether you're growing or just replacing people who quietly left.
>
> 3️⃣ No-show & cancellation cost — the silent leak. Multiply your no-show rate by an average appointment value; it's usually bigger than any discount you agonise over.
>
> None of these need software you don't already have — they're all sitting in your booking system.

### POST #4 — "Revenue's up" is dangerous
> "We're doing great — revenue's up" might be the most dangerous sentence in a small business.
>
> Because revenue going up can quietly hide:
>
> → you're growing the service that makes the least money
> → a money-loser is being subsidised by everything else
> → you're winning new patients while good ones churn out the back door
>
> Total revenue is a vanity number. It feels good and tells you almost nothing about what to do next.
>
> Profit by service, and who's coming back — that's where the real decisions live.
>
> Up compared to what? And driven by what?
>
> That's the question most owners never get a clean answer to. What number do you actually steer by?
>
> #smallbusiness #clinicmanagement #businessgrowth

## Week 3

### POST #5 — Find your most profitable service in 10 minutes
> Want to find your clinic's most profitable service in about 10 minutes? Here's the exact check:
>
> 1. Export the last 6 months of appointments + invoices.
>
> 2. For each service, get two numbers: total revenue, and the real cost to deliver it (staff time × their rate + room/materials).
>
> 3. Revenue − cost = profit per service.
>
> 4. Sort, high to low.
>
> The busiest service is very rarely the most profitable one. The one quietly carrying you is usually a surprise.
>
> Most owners have never done this even once — and when they do, it reshuffles the whole priority list.
>
> Try it this week. Tell me if the answer surprised you.
>

## Week 4

### POST #7 — The 80/20 rule is costing you
> Everyone quotes the 80/20 rule. Almost no one checks their own.
>
> "20% of our patients drive 80% of revenue." Sure — but which 20%? Most owners are guessing.
>
> When you actually pull the data, it's rarely a clean 80/20. It's 90/10. Or the "20%" you assumed turns out to be the wrong group entirely.
>
> One clinic was sure their long-time regulars carried the business. The numbers said a newer service line was doing the heavy lifting — and they'd been under-investing in it.
>
> You can't optimise a rule you've never measured.
>
> Assumed vs. measured — that's where the money quietly leaks. When did you last actually check yours?
